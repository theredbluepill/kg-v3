# X1 economic triage: summary (2026-09-30)

Sources are the item reports in this folder: `X1a.md`, `X1b.md`, `X1c.md`, `X1d.md`. Plan: `../plan.md` sections 0-2. All work ran on CPU under nice, with no training, no GPU and no repository code changes. The live learner `scratch-bank-lr2e3-4rank-20260930` had no sustained drop above 10%. X1b had one transient iteration at 4222 steps/s, about 16% below the nearby ~5010, and it recovered by the next iteration.

## What X1 established

| Item | Question | Result (exact denominators in item reports) |
|---|---|---|
| X1a | How much does the teacher (leaderboard #1) earn? | The teacher played 842 corpus games (523 won, 319 lost; win rate 62.1%). Mean bank per game: **102.8k**. Wins: 110.0k (all 523 games, from the manifest). Losses: 91.0k (16 of 319, replayed in the Rust engine; 24/24 replays matched the Kaggle banks exactly). BC best in self-play: 75.6k, which is **about 74%** of the teacher. In the 24 replayed games the teacher averaged **63** ineffective commands, against BC's 565. The teacher also produced about twice BC's harvest, water and feed volume (524/1207/367 against 248/661/185), and its sale cash was 126k against 93k. Its opponents averaged about 96-104k with 26 ineffective commands. |
| X1b | What is the ceiling when two competent sellers meet (cha22 vs cha22)? | Over 96 games (0 errors), the joint bank averaged **190.8k**, about **95.4k per seat** (SD 46k, median 180k, range 122-335k). 30 of 96 games were above the plan's 211k "bound". 89 of 96 were exact ties. On BC's evidence-econ worlds (2^62+4..7) the mirror made 85.4k per seat against BC's 75.6k, so **BC reaches about 89%**. BC beat the mirror on 2 of those 4 worlds. |
| X1c | What are the ineffective commands, and what do they cost? | 8 seeds per checkpoint. BC best: **629 per seat**. The classes were PLANT with no seeds at step start (43%), PLANT over-demand turned into PASS (32%), wrong from the start state (19%) and same-tile collision (5%). Collisions lose no harvest and cost about 0.5k per seat at most. Over-demand has an upper bound of about 15.5k per seat. A paired trim-fix run gave **-3.0k ± 9.1k** (SE), a null result at roughly 20k resolution. M@20M: 944 per seat. Its +504 per seat over BC is single-unit wrong-from-start-state commands (unready HARVEST, ineligible WATER, walking into walls), not collisions. |
| X1d | Which link of the chain breaks first (BC -> J/2 -> hz4@10M / M@10M -> M@20M)? | **Strawberry breaks first.** Strawberry tile-days: 230 (BC) and 275 (J/2), then 16 (hz4, t -4.1) and 4 (M@10M, t -4.4). The drop happens in both sibling branches, although their rewards differ. Land and sheep fall later, and harvests, sales and bank fall only at M@20M (bank 60.5k -> 36.5k per seat, t -3.6). Deaths do not lead: hz4 nearly eliminated deaths and still lost strawberry. Waste rose at J/2 while the economy grew, and it does not track the collapse. |

## What this means for the plan's branches

| Branch | X1 evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **O3, the starting economy** (X1a, X1b) | X1a lands in the plan's "teacher earns well above BC with little waste" branch: 103k against 76k, with about 9x less waste. O3's recommendation required the opposite. It asked for the #1 teacher to earn about BC's 70-80k **and** for cha22's mirror to be well above that. Neither holds. The teacher earns more than cha22's mirror per seat (103k on the ladder against 95k in the mirror), and cha22's 129-179k is mostly market share against weak rivals. | Switching to a cha22 teacher is **not supported** as a money lever by these numbers. The measured money is the gap between BC and **its existing teacher**, about 27k per seat, which is imitation fidelity. Under the plan's rule, RL is not the first lever for that gap. |
| **F5, capability** (X1c) | No waste class shows a measured cost of several thousand in bank per game. Collisions, the class that effect-aware masks and parallel frames would address, cost about 0.5k per seat. The cheap PLANT resolver's paired test was null (-3.0k ± 9.1k). | The costly unit-mask work is **not justified**. The cheap resolver is **not supported** at this resolution: it is neither confirmed nor refuted below about 20k. X1a's gap is mainly lower **production volume** (about half the teacher's harvest, water and feed), not wasted commands. So "fidelity under parallel frames" as the mechanism is not shown. |
| **M3 against M6** (X1d, X1c) | Investment (strawberry) breaks first. Deaths and waste do not lead. The waste that M@20M adds is single-unit decision error, which is drift, not execution architecture. hz4 lost strawberry under a reward that is 0.8 terminal. | This **supports M3 over M6** as the first break in the slide. M3(b) (term M scores investment as a margin loss) **cannot be the only cause**. The choice between M3(a) credit inversion and M3(c) drift that removes the rarest chains stays open, and X5 forks decide it (F3 against O1). M6 stays a baseline inefficiency, not the engine of the slide. |
| **Self-play target and denominators** (X1b, all items) | The world seed dominates the bank: SD about 46k for one deterministic policy. BC's per-seat self-play bank was 60.5k (X1d), 71.2k (X1c) and 75.6k (evidence-econ) on different 8-seed or 4-world samples. | Bank targets must be **paired, per-world ratios**. Use the plan's paired evaluation against BC on at least 32 seeds. The realistic mirror level is about 95k per seat on average, and 85k on BC's evidence worlds. The ~195-211k demand bound is **not** a ceiling. |

## What remains open

1. **Why BC earns about 74% of its teacher.** The difference is mostly volume (harvest, water and feed about 2x), not waste. Information, decision and architecture attribution is unresolved. The comparison is also not like for like: teacher ladder games against strong, varied opponents, versus BC mirror self-play on eval seeds. The teacher has no policy, so it cannot be mirrored.
2. **M3(a) against M3(c)** is unresolved. It needs X5's paired investment forks. The window between J/2 and 10M has only one checkpoint per branch, so the order inside it comes from effect size, not timing.
3. **PLANT with no seeds at step start** is 43% of BC's waste. The reading "buy and plant in the same turn, but units act before the market" is inferred from the step order, not verified.
4. **The PLANT over-demand effect below about 20k is unresolved.** Resolving it needs about 16x more seeds or a counterfactual that does not diverge.
5. **High-value worlds** (250-335k joint) are unexplained. So is whether a policy can exploit them.
6. **Coverage gaps.** Loss banks come from a 16-of-319 sample. The waste and death figures come from 24 games. X1c did not run J/2 or hz4. X1b has no per-seat revenue counters. X1b did not byte-compare step-0 snapshots against the env's.
7. **Custody.** `x1a-data/` (871 MB of episode JSONs) is local working data and must not be committed. The runner and replay crates (`x1a-replay/`, `cha22pair/`, `x1c/`) are working artifacts, not repo code. No cookbook record has been written yet. Promoting these findings (for example revising M3/M6 or O3 in the plan or cookbook) needs the note, index and log edits.
